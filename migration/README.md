# Basic’s Advanced Timer: user guide and architecture

This folder holds the whole application. The name `migration` is historical and is kept so the updater and workflow paths keep working.

## Running it

Download **BasicsAdvancedTimer-Windows-experimental** from the latest successful **Development builds** workflow run, extract the whole ZIP into its own folder and run `BasicsAdvancedTimer.Preview.exe`. The package includes the .NET runtime and needs no administrator rights. Data goes to **migration-data** beside the executable, or to the folder given with `--data-dir`. `Start-Demo.cmd` (or Settings, Open sample-data demo) runs a simulated 14:15 day with seven accounts and does not save anything real.

The right-click menu is short on purpose. It has only Collapse to tray, which tucks the timer into the notification area (or the taskbar when there is no tray) with tracking still running. Decimal hours is on the settings page, and closing the timer for good is the cross at the foot of the dashboard's rail, which turns red under the pointer. The running build (for example `Version 0.1.77 (aa17cda)`: the workflow run number and the first seven characters of the commit, or development build for local builds) and Test overtime in the sample-data demo appear once developer features are unlocked, by clicking the About tile at the foot of the settings page ten times in quick succession. Unlocking also adds a Development section to settings with the sample-data demo and a switch to hide the features again. The sample-data demo itself always has the developer features on, and shows the Development section with the switch fixed on. The version is always shown in About. Everything else is on the settings page, opened with the gear at the foot of the dashboard's icon rail.

## The two windows

- **Pill.** Timer, account and today's credit, and pause/resume. Click the account to show the list, click the timer to open the dashboard. Hold and drag anywhere along its header to move it. The expanded pill ends in a wide Start day (green) or Finish day (red) button. A thin line shows progress through the working day, turning red in overtime and amber on a break, and the pill glows amber, red or green for a break, overtime or recording.
- **Dashboard.** The dashboard opens on the Desk, a board of tiles you can move, resize and put away. A slim bar of icons along the top chooses the Desk, the Timeline or the Timesheet (whose Overview, Hours and Entries are tabs on the page), with the way back to the pill at the start, and settings and Close timer at the end. The Time tracker tile has the pause and break buttons, the Day tile has Start day and Finish day, and the Accounts tile lists the client accounts with their budgets, which can be reordered by dragging.

The big clock shows elapsed working-day time from the configured or manual start. It keeps running when accounts are paused or switched, and excludes Lunch, breaks and finished-day intervals. Time beyond the usual finish is overtime, shown in red. A day off counts only the time actually tracked. Scheduled starts take effect on their own. Start day allows early starts and resumes a finished day, and starting an account starts the day too.

## Day to day

- **Accounts.** Settings, Accounts and budgets adds, hides, reorders and recolours accounts and edits day, week, month or quarter budgets (decimals such as 7.5 are fine, blank means none). Drag a row by its grip to reorder it. The bin on a row marks that account for deleting (press it again to keep the account), and Save removes it together with everything recorded against it. An account with recorded time asks first, saves a backup copy and cannot be undone; an account with nothing recorded just goes. Hiding an account instead keeps its time. Each account has one of sixteen palette colours, or any `#RRGGBB`.
- **Break.** Stops time going to every account and wears the amber break colour. It reads Start break and Finish break. Ending a break goes back to the account that was running.
- **Working hours.** The Working hours category of Settings (the gear, which opens a dialog) edits each weekday's start, end and day-off status. Overnight shifts are not supported yet.
- **Timeline.** The hours run down a spine with a tick for each hour, and for each half and quarter hour as you zoom in. Each block shows its name, time range and credited time. On today a line and a chip in the accent colour mark the current time, with the chip's time lined up under the hour labels. While tracking, the day clock, its seconds (a lighter green), the Tracking text and dot are green on the dashboard, and the pill's clock and its line of text are green too. Scroll, or hold Ctrl and use the wheel, or use the zoom controls (fit the day, zoom out, a slider, zoom in) to zoom (60 to 640 pixels an hour). Add time, Undo and Merge sit on the left of the same bar under the timeline, with the zoom controls on its right. Drag a block to move it, or its top or bottom edge to resize it. Movement snaps to five minutes and to neighbours. Click blocks to select them and Merge to join blocks for one account and day, filling blank gaps with credited time. With one block selected, the Edit button (or Enter) opens it, and double-clicking empty space adds one. In the editor, moving a block's end later into the next block asks first, then moves the blocks in the way later (as little as needed, stopping at the first gap) as one undoable edit. Right-click for edit, merge and delete, or in empty space for Add time and Tidy day.
- **Getting started.** A new database opens on the dashboard with a four step setup over it, the dashboard blurred behind. It asks for your client accounts (rename the examples, add your own, and an optional budget in hours per day, week, month or quarter for each), your working days and hours, whether to show hours and minutes or decimal hours and which day the week starts, then lists what is worth knowing (the tray, the pill and dashboard, where the data lives) with a Start with Windows choice. Each step is applied when you press Next. Skip setup, or Esc, closes it at any point and leaves the example accounts. Settings, Getting started, Run setup again opens it again.
- **Switching back.** If you go from one account to another and back within a few minutes by mistake, Continue earlier block (in the footer under the timeline, on the pill's list, on the right-click menu and on the recording block's menu) drops the short block and carries on with the earlier one as a single block, counting the time of the slip.
- **Playing into a block ahead.** If the current time is inside a block of an account that you added or pasted ahead of time, pressing play on that account carries the block on rather than starting a second one on top of it. The block runs from its own start, keeps its note, and ends where you stop recording.
- **Pill size.** The pill is sized for the screen it is on and its Windows scale, and follows when you drag it to another screen. Settings, Pill size lets you choose a size yourself (50 to 120 per cent) or go back to Automatic.
- **Export.** The export button on the week views opens a dialog with a Week choice and a Month choice, each with a list to pick the week or month wanted (the one being viewed to begin with, back to the earliest recorded), and writes Detail.csv and Summary.csv for it.
- **Entry length.** The edit entry window has a Length box beside the start and end, so a 30 minute entry becomes 45 minutes by typing 45 (minutes), 0:45 or 0:45:00. The end follows the length and the length follows the end. When a longer entry runs into the blocks after it, you choose Move later blocks (they shift as far as they are in the way, and Undo puts everything back), Allow overlap (the entry goes in over them and the shared hours count twice) or Keep editing. Tick Always move later blocks, without asking to stop being asked, and switch it off again in Settings.
- **Copy and paste.** Right-click a block, or press Ctrl+C with blocks selected, to copy them. Right-click the timeline and choose Paste here, or press Ctrl+V, to paste with the first block at that time (Ctrl+V uses the pointer, or the end of the day's last block). Each pasted block keeps its account, credited length and note, and the gaps between blocks are kept. Blocks from one day can be copied together, and a paste is refused if it overlaps other time or runs past midnight. Like Add time it adds credited time, and Undo takes the whole paste back in one step.
- **Week views.** Week overview has three tiles (worked, breaks, unallocated), the week's chart by day and a tile listing each account with its week and budget meter. Hours table is the hours by account and day on one tile with a hairline under each row, plus the budgets on another, with copy and export beside the heading. Entries lists the week day by day, each day on its own tile with its total, each entry with its account colour, time range and credited time. Click an entry to select it, then use Edit, or right-click for edit, move, copy, merge or delete.
- **Tidy day.** Previews small gaps (up to five minutes by default), lets you give each to the previous or next account, rechecks everything on apply and adds credited time as one undoable edit.
- **Undo.** Reverses the latest time or note edit, up to 50 steps, kept across restarts.
- **Export.** Writes `Detail.csv` and `Summary.csv` to a new folder under `Timesheets/Exports` in the data folder. Decimal hours are apportioned so daily, weekly and detail figures add up exactly. Only saved entries are included, so pause first to include the running interval.
- **Icons.** Navigation, toolbar and dialog icons use rounded line strokes. The top-left pill icon includes a status dot and timer lines at rest, and on hover fills with a soft tint, widens slightly and shows the dot in the day's status colour (green while tracking, amber on a break, the accent when idle). Settings turns gently; navigation and Undo move subtly with shared 200 ms easing. Close stays still and highlights on hover. Play and pause reflect the recording state.
- **Themes.** Settings, Appearance and behaviour has a theme choice, and switches for window shadows and tooltips. System is the default and follows Windows light or dark. Light and Dark are the main built-in themes (orange accent, tinted timeline blocks with a coloured edge), joined by three studies: After Hours (deep navy with violet), Botanical (soft sage and green) and Hawthorn (forest green with lime). Edit themes opens the editor, where built-in themes can be duplicated and a copy renamed and recoloured, previewed live (themes that tint their blocks also get two sliders for how much account colour the fill takes and how strong the edge is). The editor asks before throwing away unsaved edits. Themes saved under an older name (Time Deck, Hawthorn and the rest) open as Light or Dark, whichever they were closest to. In dark themes the week chart and its legend use each account's own colour, so slices stay easy to see.
- **Taskbar, tray and start-up.** The timer has an app icon and a taskbar button, so it can be pinned and launched like any app. Settings, Show in taskbar switches the button off to run from the notification area alone, and left-clicking the tray icon then shows or hides the timer. Close timer at the foot of the dashboard rail quits the application. Alt+F4 hides the timer to the tray while tracking carries on. The tray icon is green while recording. Start with Windows adds a per-user sign-in entry and needs no administrator rights. On Windows 11 dialogs have a title bar in the theme's colours and the app icon.

## Data and safety

Records live in `timer.db` (SQLite, write-ahead log), opened exclusively so two copies cannot share a folder, with daily backups. The settings file `basics-advanced-timer.settings.json` is replaced atomically with a `.bak` copy and preserves unknown sections. The running session checkpoints every 15 seconds, stops and saves on a normal close and, after a crash, credits time only up to the last checkpoint. A failed save keeps pending time and cancels closing. The app refuses a folder that holds the old PowerShell script, and deliberately never imports the old CSV timesheets.

## Architecture

- `Timer.Core` (a NuGet package from the Basic-Timer-Core repository) is platform-independent C#: tracking engine, schedules, day calculations, credited sessions, recovery and the theme model and library.
- `Timer.Storage` (also from Basic-Timer-Core) is the SQLite database, settings, undo history, credit review, ledger editing and week export. Restoring the packages needs a `read:packages` token in the `PACKAGES_TOKEN` environment variable.
- `Timer.Desktop` is the Avalonia UI. `TimerModel` adapts the engine and store to the views. Styles and the Light/Dark/theme dictionaries come from `ThemeEngine`, which registers each theme as an Avalonia theme variant at start-up.

The build uses .NET 8 and pinned Avalonia 11.3.16.

```sh
dotnet run --project migration/src/Timer.Desktop/Timer.Desktop.csproj
dotnet test migration/tests/Timer.Desktop.Tests/Timer.Desktop.Tests.csproj --configuration Release
dotnet publish migration/src/Timer.Desktop/Timer.Desktop.csproj --configuration Release --runtime win-x64 --self-contained true -o artifacts/windows-preview
```

Tests use disposable synthetic data only. They cover the engine (early starts, pauses, lunch, finish and resume, overtime, days off, midnight splitting), storage (database, backups, undo, fault injection), the production windows and styles in a headless Avalonia, and the theme engine (contrast, derived tints, editor flow). The workflow runs them on Windows, Linux and macOS, then publishes the Windows build.
