# Basic’s Advanced Timer: engineering notes

Notes for contributors and coding assistants. The application is a C# Avalonia desktop app on .NET 8, always on top, for tracking time against client accounts and preparing timesheets. It must run without installs or administrator rights. The earlier PowerShell/WinForms app (v2.32) was removed from the tree and lives in Git history.

## Branches and releases

`main` is the Store release, `experimental` is everyday testing and `alpha` is for risky prototypes. Work on `experimental` or a `feature/...` branch from it, promote alpha to experimental and experimental to main by pull request, and never push straight to `main`. Builds show their channel beside the version, and only non-Store builds offer the sample-data demo. The preview launcher (Timer Branch Launcher, `app/preview-launcher`) serves experimental and alpha, each with its own data, versions and backups. See `BRANCHES.md`.

## Behaviour to preserve

- **Overnight meetings and Details drafts.** Shared packages are pinned at **0.10.1**. Meetings may cross calendar midnight, up to 24 hours; their end is an offset from the start date and may exceed 24 hours. Storage format **7** retains those offsets, automatically backs up the previous database before upgrade, and prevents older readers from opening the upgraded database. Meeting forms, Details, timeline drags and recurrence use the same rule. Timeline requests keep the requested calendar date; copy selection follows the configured working day. Repeating meeting drafts require Save or Discard before changing selection or day; invalid input remains available for correction. Details reports queued storage writes as unsaved until retry succeeds. See [overnight meetings and drafts](docs/design/overnight-meetings-and-drafts.md).

- **Working time and allocation.** Reports show Working time as Allocated (time on accounts) plus Unallocated (working clock time not on any account, never below zero), with Breaks separate and not counted. The running entry's elapsed time is included (`TrackingEngine.ActiveElapsedOn`). **There is no Start day or Finish day.** A day begins with its first block, recorded or added, and its clock counts up from there (`TimerModel.DaySpan`, `WorkingOn`): from the first block of the day to now while now is inside the working day (the scheduled hours, or the last recorded activity if that is later), and to the end of that stretch once it has passed, the way the engine treats a past day; the scheduled start and end only decide how far the clock runs, and a day off runs from its first block to its last. Time between blocks is Unallocated, breaks are taken out, and every Unallocated figure is `TimerModel.UnallocatedOn` (working time less what is on accounts, never below nothing), not the engine's own. The engine's Finish day (an open away stretch) is no longer offered, but an older timer's finished days keep their stretches, and such a day still ends where it was finished or at its last block, whichever is later; nothing draws a flag for it any more. Budget figures for an earlier week use that week's own period (`TimerModel.BudgetReference`, `BudgetUsedAt`, `BudgetPeriodNameAt`, the Thursday rule), never "this month". Hiding an account (In tracker) only removes it from the tracker list. Its time stays in `ReviewAccounts` and every report. Scroll views draw no line, fade or shadow at their edges (a hairline at the top and bottom of scrolling content was tried and taken out, `Ui.WithMoreBelow` is gone).
- **Colour roles.** Green (`Live`) means tracking only, the accent means selection, focus and the main action (orchid, in two tones: the pale `Accent`, `#D3B0EA` in Light and `#CBA9E8` in Dark, which carries washes and the ink-on-accent buttons (`AccentInk`), and `AccentLit`, derived by `ThemeEngine.AccentLit` from the same hue, which draws lamps, selection rings, the now line, pins, focus edges, accent outlines and thin strokes; it holds 3.3 to 1 on the page, the card and a sunk well, which the pale tone does not on Light. Both stay clear of red, green and orange), amber means a break, `BudgetWarning` means a budget at 80 per cent and red (`Overtime`, a token name kept from when there was overtime) means a spent budget or an overdue reminder. There is no overtime: time worked past the day's hours is simply working time, never a separate figure, colour or state. A budget bar with room left is neutral (`Muted`), the setup's current step dot is the accent, and the idle pill dot is `Idle`.
- **Duration format.** Every duration reads as HH:MM (`TimerModel.Format`, `FormatShort` and `FormatCompact` all give 01:30), and as decimal hours when that is chosen. Do not add a new way of writing one.
- **Credited totals.** Entries hold credited durations. Totals come from credited seconds, never from clock spans. Positioning credit never adds hours; "Add time" does.
- **Day clock.** The main clock is the day's working time so far (`TimerModel.Elapsed`, `WorkingOn(now)`), counting up from the first block of the day and excluding breaks, so it reads 00:00:00 until something is recorded or added and then ticks on through quiet time as well as through recording. Stopping or switching an account does not reset it. There is no Start day or Finish day and no day item in the tray menu. `SetShifts` drops manual starts the new hours make unnecessary. `ResumeAfterBreak` is stored with the running row. `BreakAccount.Name = "Break"` is reserved.
- **Exact decimal hours.** Exports apportion hundredths of an hour by largest remainder, first across account/day totals and then within each bucket, so every sum is exact. Running totals are provisional until saved.
- **Gaps.** Selected gaps are validated against the unrounded current gap before being moved to whole seconds, because durations use hundredths of a minute.
- **Switching accounts.** A block's length is kept to hundredths of a minute and its start to the second, so `TrackingEngine.ToggleAccount` starts the new block at the whole second after the old one's stored end when needed, and consecutive blocks never overlap. The timeline also treats blocks within two seconds as meeting, so older overlaps do not split the day into lanes. After a quick slip from one account to another and back (a block of three minutes or less, switched back within three minutes, `TrackingEngine.ContinuableBlocks`), `ContinuePrevious` drops the short block and reopens the earlier one so it carries on as one, with the slip's time. It is offered in the floating message (`MessageBar`, its `ContinuePrevious` button) for a few minutes, and not for blocks in a group. Making it automatic with an Undo (brief item 57) needs the engine to undo a continue, so the offer stays for now. Pressing play on an account whose block the time now falls inside (one added or pasted ahead of time) carries that block on instead of starting a second one on top of it (`TrackingEngine.FutureBlockOf`, `ContinuedBlock`): it is reopened from its own start, stopping ends it where the recording stops, and it is skipped for a block in a group or one that would overlap other recorded time. A break is carried on the same way, so starting a break inside a break block already there reopens it.
- **Selecting and editing timeline blocks.** A plain click selects just that block (`TimerModel.SelectBlock`), and clicking the only selected block again clears the selection; Shift-click adds a block to the selection or takes it out (that is how two blocks are picked to merge). There is no double-click to edit, which could not be told apart from two clicks. A selected block is edited in the Details card under the timeline (`SelectedBlockCard`, which keeps the name it had as the Selected block card; its title is Details, with a chip beside it naming what it shows: Block, Blocks, Merged · 2 blocks, Meeting, Meetings, or N blocks selected (no counts: the timeline above and the stack under it show them); see Details below). Its header is a grid with one stretching row at a fixed height, so its title stays where it is whether none, one or several blocks are selected. There is no Edit button and no Enter shortcut. Right-click menus on a block carry the other actions. Double-clicking empty space still adds time. The block being recorded is not selectable (a click says it is still recording). **Keys.** Ctrl+Z undoes the last edit (`TimerModel.UndoEdit`), Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z puts it back (`TimerModel.RedoEdit`; `LedgerEditor.Redo`, added in the shared package 0.6.0, kept in memory only and ended by any new edit, account rename or delete, or a change to the ledger since) and + or - lengthens or shortens the one selected block by a minute, moving its end and keeping its start (`TimerModel.NudgeSelection`, one undo step each; Shift is allowed since + needs it on most keyboards, and a block can’t go below a minute or beyond 24 hours; several blocks or a merged block are refused in the toast). Both work on the Timeline page only, never while a box has the focus (which keeps its own undo) or a dialog is open, and the bar's Undo and Redo are icon buttons alone (`UndoEdit`, `RedoEdit`, `IconKind.Undo`, `IconKind.Redo`, enabled by `CanUndo` and `CanRedo`) whose tooltips name the edit and the key (Undo delete entry, Ctrl Z; Redo delete entry, Ctrl Y), as the toast's Undo shows Ctrl Z beside its tooltip.
- **Dragging, the magnet and overlap.** Time may overlap everywhere: dragging, resizing, typing in Details, Add time and Paste all allow a block to share hours with others (they stack in lanes), and only the block being recorded refuses it. A drag has a magnet (`TimelineGestures.Magnet`, the board "Timeline behaviours"): the edge being moved is pulled flush to the nearest edge of another block, or to now, within `TimelineGestures.MagnetPixels` (10 screen pixels at any zoom, so about 6 minutes at the default zoom and under 4 at 160 an hour), so most drags butt blocks together with no gap, and past that band it goes where the pointer is, to the 5 minutes, and may overlap; nothing is pushed. Alt turns the magnet off and moves by the minute; Shift is the old insert, which does push the neighbours along (`SnapDelta` with `insert`, `PlanTimeline`, `TimerModel.DragEntry`), and was automatic once a drag went 24 pixels into a neighbour. While dragging, an accent line marks the seam it snapped to and a chip says Flush with the time, or how much it now overlaps (`TimelineSnapLine`, `TimelineSnapChip`); a plain drag is committed by `TimerModel.MoveEntry` (an edit that allows overlap). A block's end is kept to the whole second and its length to hundredths of a minute, so a snap to a neighbour's start goes through `LastDeltaBefore`, which steps back a second at a time until the saved block really ends no later than it, and a fractional end is rounded up so a snap never leaves a sliver of overlap. This matters most for the block being recorded, whose start has a fraction of a second.
- **Day starts at (overnight work).** A day is the 24 hours from a time the user chooses, midnight by default (`TimerModel.DayStart`, saved as the appearance line `daystart=HH:mm`, set in Settings, Day and time by the `DayStartBox` (a time box and nothing else: the canned choices Midnight, 12:00 and 18:00 are gone), and pushed to `Engine.DayStart` and `Notes.DayStart`; the board "Timeline behaviours", Timer Core 0.9.0). Someone on nights sets it to, say, 18:00, so a 22:00 to 06:00 shift is one block on one day on one page. **Which day:** a block counts on the day it starts (`TimerModel.CountsOn`, `Engine.WorkDayOf`), a moment belongs to the day that holds it (`DayOf`, `Notes.DayOf`), "today" is `TodayKey` (after midnight a night shift is still yesterday's day), and `DayBegins` and `DayEnds` are the moments a day begins and ends. Everything per day follows it: the Timeline, the clock, `DaySpan`, `WorkingOn`, `AllocatedOn`, `UnallocatedOn`, `BreakTimeOn`, the account rows (`Today`, `OnDay`, budgets through `Engine.Credited`), the Week overview's columns, the exports and the alerts. Rows keep their calendar date, may cross midnight (up to 24 hours: `LedgerEditor.MaxBlock`) and are cut by a recording at the day's beginning instead of midnight. The Timeline runs from the day's beginning for 24 hours (`TimelineHour_<n>` is the n-th hour from there, labelled with the real clock time), midnight is a quiet dashed marker with the new weekday (`TimelineMidnight`, `TimelineMidnightLabel`) and a block that began before the day is drawn from its edge; the header names the stretch ("Mon 3 Nov, 18:00 – Tue 06:00") only when the day does not begin at midnight. A note or meeting is a moment on its own date (`NotesModel.MomentOf`, a dated note with no time at 09:00) and shows on the day that holds it (`MeetingsOn`, `PinnedOn`, `ForDay`; `ReviewViews.MeetingStart` and `MeetingEnd`). An end typed earlier than the start is the next day's time (`TimeText.ParseClockAfter`, used by Details and Add time), so 22:00 to 02:00 is four hours. Dragging stays inside the day on show and never pulls a block further out than it already is (`ClampToDay` with the day's beginning). The engine's own overtime and shift maths (`Totals`) is not used.
- **Undo.** Edits are stored as splices plus hashes in `edits` and `edit_changes`, so history stays small and survives restarts.

## Storage

Notes keeps failed series creation, updates and deletion in its retry state alongside note writes. Negative series IDs are temporary and are resolved before dependent notes are saved. `TimerModel.SaveError` combines ledger failures with pending Notes persistence. A date or repeat change for this and later meetings ends the old series before the original occurrence and starts a new series from the moved occurrence.

Plain-note selection (`SelectedNoteId`) uses the same commit validation as blocks and meetings. Recovered note IDs update the selection, inline editor state and Details field IDs before the model refreshes, so a focused draft stays attached to its note.

Selection navigation calls `CommitSelection` before dropping the old selection, and remaps row positions after an edit replaces a row. Timeline and stack gestures use `BeginTimelineGesture`/`EndTimelineGesture` so checkpoints defer rebuilding the grabbed controls. Details and account lists use `ViewActivity` to pause hidden work. Notes increments `Revision` for changes so the Notes page can skip same-minute clock updates.

`TimerDatabase` holds accounts, entries, groups, away, day_starts, running, edits, edit_changes and meta. It takes an exclusive lock, uses WAL and `user_version`, and makes daily backups. The schema is format 2 (a budget can be per day), and `TimerDatabase.Upgrades` holds the numbered steps that take an older file forward after a copy is saved; a step that rebuilds a table runs with foreign keys off and is checked with `foreign_key_check` before it is kept, and an older build refuses a newer file. Dispose every `SqliteCommand` or Windows keeps the file locked, which fails tests on CI. `TimerStore` rewrites `entries` when a snapshot changes. Notes, groups and `accountsSeeded` are blobs in `meta`. The settings JSON preserves unknown sections and is swapped in atomically with a `.bak`. Sessions checkpoint every 15 seconds and recovery stops at the last checkpoint. `TimerStore` refuses a folder holding the old `AccountTimer2.ps1` or launcher, and the old CSV files are deliberately never imported. Never auto-open another app's data folder. Never commit `migration-data`, `timer.db`, settings JSON or logs (`.gitignore` excludes them).

## Themes

Each theme is a `ThemeDefinition` (name, Light or Dark base, colour tokens, block style) and an Avalonia `ThemeVariant`. `ThemeTokens.Names` lists the tokens (Surface, Panel, Ink, Muted, Line, Hover, Selected, Live, Idle, Overtime, BudgetWarning, Break, Finish, Start, Accent, AccentInk, Clock, PillSurface, PillInk, PillBreak, PillOvertime, ShadowColor). `ThemeEngine.Register` writes brushes into `Application.Resources.ThemeDictionaries` in place so open windows repaint. Light and Dark register in `App.Initialize`. The pill (`PillWindow`, in `PillWindow.cs`) is a small card, 406 by 72 with the dashboard's 20 pixel corner (so the pill grows into the dashboard without its corner changing, and the account list and alerts cards take the same corner) (`PillWindow.PillWidth`, `PillHeight`, `PillRadius`), with the room around it for its shadow or glow (`Ui.ShadowMargin`). It is the only shape: the vertical pill, its account drawer, and the choice of shape in the getting started setup and in Settings are gone, and a saved `pillshape=` line is ignored and left alone in the settings file. Along it, centred on its height, from `PillWindow.InsetLeft` (10, level with the 10 above and below the display) to `InsetRight` (14): inside it: a recessed display window (`StatusFace.Display`, `PillDisplay`, 52 tall like the well beside it, radius 14, padding 10, `DisplayWidth` fixed so nothing in it ever changes the width; filled with `DisplayWell` and drawn with an inset shadow `DisplayShade` and a hairline lip `DisplayEdge`, all written by `ThemeEngine.Write`) holding, left to right: the state LED (`PillStateLight`, a capsule 6 by 14 with radius 3.5, the F3 look: with the timer idle it is an empty groove (`DisplayGroove`), and while recording or on a break it is inlaid in the state colour with an inset shade, no separate slot; its tooltip and accessible name say Recording, On break or Not recording), a divider, the day clock as seven-segment digits (`SegmentClock`, below; the tile is the `PillDashboard` button and opens the dashboard), a divider, and the alert count (`PillAlertCount`, below). Beside it, 12 pixels apart, one soft well (`PillWell`, the text colour at about 5 per cent over the card, 7 on a dark theme, 4 pixels of padding and 2 between its tiles) holding the primary control (`PillPrimaryTile`, 46 by 44 with a 14 pixel corner) and the account's box (`PillAccounts`, whose content is `PillAccountSelector`: the state's dot beside a quiet chevron, no letters, 48 wide, bare on the well until the pointer is over it) and, 12 pixels on at the far end, the break control (`PillBreak`, a `BreakButton`, its tile `StatusFace.TileSize` 44 with `TileRadius` 14). **The segment clock** (`SegmentClock.cs`): `SegmentShapes` builds the sixteen slanted, round-capped bars of a digit (the account name's table, slant and bar proportions, a 19 wide cell holding a 13.4 glyph at 28 tall; the colon is two leaning dots) for any height once and keeps them, `SegmentRun` draws a run of digits, blanks and colons from them, and `SegmentClock` is the day clock, `ClockMain` (hours and minutes, in the text colour) and `ClockSeconds` (a colon and two digits, in the quiet colour but the same height), digits `SegmentClock.DigitHeight` 28 tall. It is always two digit cells, the leading one blank under ten and both blank with nothing waiting (and with Pill alerts off), capped at 99, so the display's width never changes; a new arrival flashes it twice (`StatusFace.FlashCount`; steady with less motion asked for). On the pill it is a button (`PillAlertsButton`) that opens the alerts card. The right-click menu is `Ui.SettingsMenu`. **Alerts on the pill** (Round 28, the board "Pill notifications: moving between them"). A save that failed is not an alert, but the pill shows it in a toast of its own (`PillWindow.PillToast`, a `MessageBar` made with `failuresOnly`, so it says only a failed save and never notes, refusals or the continue offer; hung above the pill in its own pop-up, `PillToastPopup`, or below it when there is no room above; it stays until a save works or the cross is pressed). `TimerModel.Save` tells the model when a failure clears, so the toast goes at once. The whole pill is drawn at 95 per cent of its design size at most, and smaller on a narrow screen (below about 1700 device independent pixels) or a low Windows scale (100 per cent gives 80 per cent, 125 per cent or more gives 95 per cent), because a display with fewer pixels to the inch makes the same pill look bigger (`PillWindow.ScaleFor`, applied by `FitToScreen` through a layout transform). The size is worked out again whenever the pill is shown, when it is dragged onto another screen (`FitIfScreenChanged`, on position changes) and when Windows changes a screen's scale. A size chosen in Settings, Pill size (`TimerModel.PillScalePercent`, 50 to 120 per cent, saved as `pill=NN` or `pill=auto` in the appearance lines) overrides it and applies at once, even while the pill is hidden; with no screen to measure the size is left as it is. The window is always brought to the size the scale calls for by `PillWindow.EnsureSize`, never only when the scale moves (that left a white card under the capsule when the scale was set before the window showed), and it is run after every scale change, when the window opens, when its real size is wrong, and a quarter of a second after the last move of a drag, because Windows ignores size changes during the drag and a pill dragged to another screen would otherwise keep its old window size. The default is System (`ThemeLibrary.Default`), which is Light or Dark by the computer's light or dark setting (`TimerModel.CurrentTheme` resolves it and re-resolves when Windows changes; the app always requests an explicit variant). Two themes ship (`AppThemes.BuiltIn`): Light and Dark, offered with System and Sunset as four small pictures in Settings, Appearance. **Sunset** (`AppThemes.Sun`, saved as the theme `Sun`) is a choice and not a third theme: `TimerModel.CurrentTheme` is Dark from sunset to sunrise and Light by day (`SunIsDark`, `SunDay`), found again whenever the model repaints, so the 15 second tick changes it at the boundary without a timer of its own. The times come from `SunTimes` (the almanac formula, a few minutes' accuracy, worked in UTC and shown in `TimerModel.SunZone`, the computer's zone; no network and no location service, since the app needs no installs) for a city on a bundled list (`SunTimes.Cities`, each with its standard offset, so the guess from the computer's zone, `SunTimes.Guess`, lands on a city in it) or for two times typed in (`SunTimes.ByHand`; also the fallback on a day with no sunrise or sunset). They are the appearance lines `suncity=<city or hand>` and `sunhand=HH:mm-HH:mm`; the controls (`SunOptions`, `SunPlace`, `SunDarkFrom`, `SunLightFrom`, `SunSummary`) show under the tiles only while Sunset is chosen. After Hours, Botanical, Hawthorn and a user's own themes are retired for now: a saved choice of one becomes Light or Dark by its base (`AppThemes.Resolve`, applied in the `TimerModel` constructor), and the theme itself is left alone in the settings file. Light is `AppThemes.Light`, the shared package's Light made cool and crisp (Round 28, option B on the canvas: page `#EDF0F5`, cards `Panel` white, lines `#D5DBE5`, hovers `#E4E9F1`, text `#131B2A`, quiet text `#4E5A6C`, accent `#D3B0EA` with `AccentInk` `#241B36`, selection `#EEE0F7` (Dark has accent `#CBA9E8`, `AccentInk` `#0E1422` and selection `#43364F`; the lit tone is derived, not stored), and blocks that take 78 per cent of their account's colour (`BlockTint`) so they stand clear of the grey tiles behind them; the Break token is taken a little darker only as far as it needs to hold 3 to 1 on the page, `AppThemes.BreakOnTheCoolPage`), and the shared package, which the desk app also uses, is unchanged. `ThemeEngine.Write` writes the standard menu resources (`MenuFlyoutPresenterBackground`, the item hover and pressed backgrounds, text, disabled text and separator) from Panel, Hover, Selected, Ink, Muted and Line, so right-click menus follow the theme, and `App.axaml` rounds them (`ContextMenu` 12, items 8). Combo boxes carry an even padding (`App.axaml` overrides Fluent's 12,5,0,7, which sat the words above the middle). Text boxes and combo boxes follow the mock's field look the same way (`TextControl...` and `ComboBox...` resources: a fill a shade off the card, a hairline, the accent when focused; radius 11 and 12 in `App.axaml`). It also writes `ScrollThumb` and `ScrollThumbHover` (the line colour mixed towards the text colour), which the one slim scroll bar style in `App.axaml` uses everywhere (10 pixels wide, an 8 pixel rounded thumb, no arrows, a transparent track, fading in when the pointer is over the area). `ThemeLibrary.Renamed` maps names that were renamed or removed (Studio, Time Deck, Sage, Ocean and so on) onto Light or Dark by their base. Only a missing theme setting defaults to `ThemeLibrary.Default`; a saved choice is kept. Tests require Ink on Surface and Panel at 7:1, Muted on Surface at 3.5:1 and AccentInk on Accent at 4.5:1 for every built-in, and Ink at 4.5:1 and Muted at 3:1 on the pill's state colours. Writing a theme (`ThemeEngine.Write`) replaces only the resources whose colour changed, because replacing one makes every control using it look again. Timeline blocks are drawn by the theme's block rules (`ThemeDerivation.Block`, via `TimerModel.BlockLook`): the fill is the account colour solid, softened or tinted (`BlockTint` is how much account colour the fill takes), with a gradient to a deeper end (see the timeline paragraph for the corners). Light and Dark both tint their blocks. Charts use `TimerModel.ChartColour`, which is the account's own colour in dark themes because tinted fills sink into the dark surface. Headings and figures use Inter, the interface face, set heavier with equal-width figures (`+tnum`); the old Bricolage Grotesque fonts are gone. The one font file in `Assets/Fonts` is Chakra Petch SemiBold (SIL Open Font License, `OFL.txt` beside it, compiled in as an `AvaloniaResource`) for the plain clock digits. `ClockFont`, `FiguresFont` and `ClockLightFont` stay as resource keys pointing at Inter, and the `clock`, `figures` and `clockLight` classes set the weights (Bold, SemiBold, Medium).

## The depth look (Round 34)

The whole app is drawn as raised and sunk surfaces on warm greys, from one set of theme resources written by `ThemeEngine.Write` (`ShadowCard`, `ShadowKey`, `ShadowKeyHover`, `ShadowSunk`, `ShadowPopup`, `ShadowThumb`, the brushes `Well`, `KeyHover`, `Porcelain`, `KeyEdge`, and the text shades `LiveText` and `BreakText`). **The grammar:** raised means pressable (a key: `Button.ctl`, `toolButton`, `accountPill`, the pill's round keys), a hover lifts it a step (`KeyHover`, `ShadowKeyHover`) and a press goes in (`Well`, `ShadowSunk`); sunk and coloured means on (a chosen view tab or option, a chosen account pill, the break while it is on); sunk and neutral means a value or a place to type (fields and combo boxes are wells with an inset shadow and a faint `KeyEdge`, the slider tracks, the pill's well, a selected row). Buttons set `BoxShadow` on their `ContentPresenter#PART_ContentPresenter`, text boxes on `Border#PART_BorderElement`, combo boxes on `Border#Background`. **Sliders** (`Border.viewTabs`/`Button.viewTab`, `Border.segTrack`/`Button.seg`) are a sunk track with a raised thumb (`ShadowThumb`) under the chosen option, its words in `AccentText`, for the three views and every either-or choice. **Light is softer than Dark** (`ThemeEngine.Write`, `lightGround`): half the shadow strength (8.5 per cent against 17), tinted lavender-grey (96, 88, 132) instead of cool grey so it sits in the page, the card shadow pulled in (6 and 8 pixels down and across, 24 of blur), and every sunk surface (`ShadowSunk`: text boxes, tracks, chosen tabs) a shallower, wider inset (1 and 1.5 pixels, 5 of blur, at 60 per cent), so a field reads as cut in rather than dug out; Dark keeps its shadows. **Cards** (`:is(Border).dayCard`) are raised (`ShadowCard`) with the corner scale: card 18, well or field 12, control 11, small 9, block 10, chip 6 (the pill keeps 20 so it grows into the dashboard unchanged). **Scroll bars** lie over the content, so every list that scrolls (the bell's and pill's lists, the Notes card and page, the Notes views, the Details rows) calls `Ui.LeaveRoomForTrack`, which gives its rows 14 pixels on the right only while it scrolls. **Menus** and hanging cards are porcelain (`Porcelain`, `ShadowPopup`) with the row under the pointer sunk. **The toast** (`MessageBar`) is a porcelain card with a sunk round medallion holding the state's tick or mark in `LiveText`, `BreakText` or `Overtime`, and no countdown bar (the model still times the message out); `CountdownFill` is gone. **Shadows are drawn outside the thing that casts them, so nothing above it may clip** (`ShadowClipTests` checks every page, dialog and the pill; a `ContentControl` clips by default, so one that hosts raised keys needs `ClipToBounds = false`, as `NotesForm`, `NotesTitle` and `PageForm` have, and a scroll view needs room round its content, as the Settings card has): `Button` and the dashboard's `workspace` content control clip to their own rectangle by default, which cut every key's and card's shadow off square (`App.axaml` turns the button clip off with `:is(Button)`, since a plain `Button` selector skips a subclass such as `BreakButton`, which kept its shadow cut square; `workspace.ClipToBounds = false`), and lists of raised rows are inset (`AccountList` margin, `RowGap`) so a scroll area does not clip them. **Pairs and places**: Undo and Redo, and zoom out and in, are one raised piece each (`Ui.KeyPair`, `keyPair`, a hairline between, each half its own button); the timeline's day sits in a sunk trough (`TimelineTrough`, `Border.trough`, with 6 pixels of room above the hour labels and a soft shadow at its left and right edges, `TimelineLipLeft` and `TimelineLipRight`, so the day reads as lying below the lip) with meetings and stack tiles as small raised cards on it (`MeetBg` is Panel, `ShadowThumb`); account rows are raised cards with a round raised play key drawn by `AccountIndicator` (only the key sinks while it records; the row stays raised with a green wash); note rows are raised cards with a raised round tick, the importance star is a small neutral key with an outlined star whose stroke turns gold when on (see The controls gallery), tick boxes are 20 pixel circles like the notes tick (raised when empty, sunk when checked or mixed), and the theme pictures are raised keys with the chosen one sunk and ringed. **The pill and sidebar**: the play and pause key is a round raised key (`StatusFace.TileSize` 44, `TileRadius` 22) standing in a sunk capsule well (`WellRadius` 26) with the account selector, and the break is a round raised key outside the well. A key that is on is sunk into a well with its symbol in its role colour, as the mock draws it: the play key while an account records (the symbol in green) and the break while a break is on (the cup in the break orange, no solid fill); the sidebar's tracking card uses the same parts, with the break key outside the well on the right (`StatusFace` builds a `track` grid of well and break), exactly as on the pill. **Account colours** come from `AccountTones`: `Vivid` for timeline blocks (white words, dark words on pale yellows) and `Chart` for charts (chalky on Light, lifted on Dark), so the week chart's slices stay flat. Reminder pop-up: iOS rows with a time wheel (see Notes and meetings); the Meeting day card is 222 wide so its month is centred. **Frame timing** (`FrameMonitor`, Settings, About, Development, and a chip at the dashboard's bottom right) records the gaps between frames (average, 95th percentile, worst, how many over 20 and 33 ms, and how the frames spread across 17.5, 20, 25 and 33 ms) so performance can be checked on Windows. Two developer switches beside it help find what costs frames: Draw flat (`ThemeEngine.FlatShadows`, every raised and sunk shadow written empty) and Pause the 1 s refresh (`TimerModel.TickPaused`); neither is saved, and the report names the one that was on. A page's figures start again on arriving at it, so the monitor keeps the figures of each page it is left (`FrameMonitor.Remember`) and Copy report gives a line for every page visited since it started, which makes the Timeline measurable from Settings. Each line also ends with what happened on that page per second (`FrameMonitor.Bump`: layout passes, model changes, clock ticks, timeline builds, name sliding, and how often each custom-drawn control redrew), so a page that quietly lays out or redraws something every frame stands out against one that does not. The report also names Windows' system timer tick (`TimerResolution`, 15.6 ms by default, which paces timer-driven frames: the Windows readings showed a p95 of about 31 ms, two ticks, on every page and in every mode, and a page's fps changing with whatever else on the computer had asked for a finer tick), and a developer switch asks Windows for a 1 ms tick to see what that does; neither is saved.

## The pill's display (Round 35)

The pill is one display window and a 6 pixel bezel on all sides (`PillWindow.BezelWidth`). The display fills the inset area so any fractional width rounding goes into its well. The outer corner radius is the display radius plus the bezel width. The pill's display well uses a centred inset shadow and an even lip, so the shading does not add visual weight to one edge. There are **no dividers**. The state LED, clock, alert count and six-cell account name lead to one 34 pixel column. Play/pause sits above the boxed **BREAK** label, with separate hit zones and a 1 pixel gap. The two glyphs span 31 pixels beside the clock's 28 pixel digits. The sidebar's display stays 212 by 100. Its clock row is unchanged, and the lower row uses the recovered width for a ten-cell account name beside the same control column. The pill's compact mode omits both badges and the account name as before.

**One honeycomb across the whole display** (`Honeycomb` in `DisplayDots.cs`): the same tiled 1.45 px hexagon mask covers the clock and count, name, LED, bell and both badges. `TransportGlyph` and `BreakBadgeGlyph` use `VfdGlyph` with the same 30 by 14 bounds, 1.4 px outline stroke and bloom outside the mesh. Lit clock digits, colons, the alert count and the bell use the same bloom. The bell stays dim when no alerts are waiting. The transport cross-fades between triangle and bars and turns amber during a break. The break badge is muted at rest and brightens in amber on a break, hover or keyboard focus. `SteppedControl` only runs its timer while an animation is in progress and the control is attached. Reduced motion disables the fades and name scrolling. Longer account names loop one cell every 380 ms, with three blank cells between repeats. `SegmentName.ForAccount` normalises names and falls back to `ACCT nn` when too little can be drawn. The full account name remains available in the tooltip. All five controls keep the `Button.pillBare.zone` hover and pressed washes.

The clock and alert count always use segmented digits. Tray, Reset position still refits and places it.

## UI conventions and pitfalls

- **Pill list rows.** Pressing anywhere on an account row, in the pill's list and the sidebar alike, starts or stops tracking that account (decided: the whole row, not only the glyph).
- **Glass timeline.** The timeline's trough (`TimelineTrough`) is dark glass in both looks: its content sits in a `ThemeVariantScope` that asks for Dark, so every theme resource inside resolves dark, and code that draws colours there reads `TimerModel.GlassTheme` (the Dark theme), never `CurrentTheme`. Blocks keep their vivid account colours. A selected, recording or ringed block, tile or meeting wears `ReviewViews.Ring` (the 3 pixel ring first, then a soft bloom) and the now line glows. A graticule of three faint horizontal rules (`TimelineGraticuleTop`, `Middle` and `Foot`, tagged `Graticule`) lies on the band's top, middle and foot behind everything. Outlined blocks are not built.
- **Soft calendar.** `SoftDatePicker` (the header's date calendar) and the reminder's month draw each day through `SoftDatePicker.DayContent`: the number, and a small dot in the corner on a day that holds time (`DayTimeDot`, from `SoftDatePicker.DayHasTime`, set to `WorkingOn(day) > 0` on the header). Saturday and Sunday carry the `weekendDay` class and read in the quiet colour.
- **Latches.** A checked tick draws its mark and ring in `AccentLit`; Merge (a momentary key) uses the `accentText` class when it can act, never the filled primary look.
- **Notes filter and kind.** The Notes page kind (`PageKind`) is a Note/Meeting slider (`PageKindNote`, `PageKindMeeting`). The account filter is one button (`NotesAccountFilter`) hanging a card of accounts with a tick, a dot and a count each (`NotesAccount_<name>`, any number at once, `NotesAccountClear`). Export period has `ExportPeriodEarlier` and `ExportPeriodLater` keys either side of its list.
- **Choice strips.** `ChoiceStrip` (`Choices.cs`) replaces a drop-down where the options are few: `ChoiceLook.Chips` (a lamp, an optional account-colour dot, `Button.ctl.chip`; folds past `FoldAfter` 8 into "+N more"), `Segments` (the `segTrack` slider look, for either-or and repeat) and `Days` (short weekday chips) and `Dropdown` (a combo box with the colour dots, used for the account in Details, Add time and the Notes page, where a row of chips took too much room). It mirrors the ComboBox members the app used (`ItemsSource`, `SelectedItem`, `SelectedIndex`, `SelectionChanged`). Drop-downs stay for long or dynamic lists (the account fields, budget period in dense rows, `SunPlace`, `ExportPeriodPick`, `SeasonPreview`). The dashboard is 1272 by 800 so Account can have its own row in Details.

- Pop-ups are dialogs, not windows. `Dialog` (in `Dialogs.cs`) is a `ContentControl` with a Title, Width and a `Close()`, shown by the dashboard's `DialogHost` (`DashboardWindow.Dialogs`) on a card over a dim scrim, with the dashboard blurred and unclickable behind it, like the getting started setup. They stack, and Escape or the cross closes the top one before it clears a selection. Every pop-up is one (`EntryDialog`, which is Add time only, `AccountsDialog` and `ShiftDialog`); only the pill and the dashboard are windows. The host draws the card's header (the dialog's Title on the left and the cross on the right) above the dialog's own content, so a dialog does not repeat its title and a scroll bar never runs under the cross. Buttons inside dialogs use `Ui.DialogButton`, which gives the dashboard's quiet outlined look (`toolButton quiet`) and an accent outline on the main action (`mainAction`, for Save, Export and the like). A dialog can refuse to close through its `Closing` event, for one that wants to ask about unsaved edits first. Their height follows their content up to the window. In tests, `TestDialogs.Open` shows a dialog over a dashboard. A hidden dashboard shows no dialog, so the pill's Name and budget menu item asks the model (`TimerModel.OpenAccounts`) and the dashboard comes up first (`DashboardWindow.Reveal`).
- Headings in pop-ups, the settings page and the dashboard use `Ui.Heading`, the `clock` display face, and every button in a pop-up or on the settings page is a `Ui.DialogButton`. ## Keeping builds few

Each merge into `experimental` or `alpha` builds and stores a Windows ZIP (kept 14 days), and a pull request run adds tests on Windows. A manual run of the workflow (Run workflow, or the API) on any other branch keeps its own ZIP for 7 days, named for the branch, so a feature branch can be tried without a merge. The owner tests only every few versions, so batch work: keep related changes on one `feature/...` branch, open one pull request for the lot, and sync `experimental` only for a candidate. Before opening a pull request for a small change, ask whether it can wait for the next one. Rendered previews upload only when a test fails or a run is started by hand.

**Timer Branch Launcher** (`app/preview-launcher`, built from `TimerBranchLauncher.csproj`) replaced the old updater, and `migration/updater` is gone. It is a standalone self-contained Windows x64 executable that embeds its install worker (`UpdaterWorker.ps1`). You pick Alpha or Experimental, or type a preview branch name, and it downloads that branch's latest available portable build and launches it; Launch installed and Roll back and launch work offline. Downloads and the branch list use the GitHub CLI (`gh`), which it looks for even when the launcher's PATH is stale, with a GitHub sign-in button. The default install folder is `%LOCALAPPDATA%\BAT Launcher`; placing it beside an older `test-install` folder, or choosing that folder's parent, reuses those builds and data. Each branch keeps its own data directory, and rollback switches executables after a data backup without touching the database format. The launcher is not built on every run: a manual run of the development workflow with **Include the Timer Branch Launcher download** builds it and uploads `Timer-Branch-Launcher-Windows` for 14 days, while every pull request tests the install and rollback logic offline (`Test-UpdaterWorker.ps1`) and runs the packaged executable's `--smoke-test`. Its guide is `app/preview-launcher/README.md`.

**The controls gallery** (`NeumorphicGalleryWindow`, `NeumorphicGallerySamples`, `GalleryMaterialStudy`, Settings, About, Development, Open controls gallery; the demo has Development on already) compares Light and Dark side by side without changing the selected theme, using Avalonia's `ThemeVariantScope`. Its samples are not given the timer model, so they never write preferences or touch tracking. The sections are States, Existing buttons, On surfaces, Menus and navigation, Ticks and inputs and Colour experiments (the supplied Sage, Lavender, Butter, Clay, Coral, Ink, Mint, Blush and Slate palette, not assigned to anything yet). The shared styles live in `Styles/NeumorphicControls.axaml` (isolated Button and ToggleButton themes and the circular CheckBox theme, with `NeumorphicControls` making the controls; the generic Button styles exclude them), the gallery-only materials in `Styles/GalleryMaterialStyles.axaml`, and `ThemeEngine` derives the local `Neo*` resources from each theme's colours (they follow live theme changes and the Draw flat switch). What has reached the working pages is small: the outlined importance star on the note lists and composers, disabled primary buttons in the neutral grey with no shadow, and circular native checkboxes. Everything else in the gallery is a comparison, and the gallery's `previewHover`, `previewPressed` and `previewFocus` classes show states without real interaction. The material rules and the review checks are in `docs/design/neumorphic-controls.md` and `docs/design/neumorphic-gallery-study.md`; `NeumorphicControlsTests` covers the gallery and keyboard activation, and the snapshot tests wait for real presented frames through the public frame pump. The timeline's pointer guide runs to the bottom of the display, including when snapped to a reminder pin.

## Checking changes

Run `dotnet test app/tests/Timer.Desktop.Tests -c Release`. CI runs it on Windows only and publishes a self-contained Windows build; check the run before saying tests pass. A test that fails now and then is a defect to find, never to re-run past: **Repeat the tests (manual)** (`test-repeat.yml`: Run workflow with a number of times, available once the file is on `main`; a pull request that changes the file runs it too, eight times) runs the whole suite again and again on one build and lists which tests failed in which runs. Headless input is hit-tested against the last rendered frame, so the tests' `RobustFrames` and `RobustInput` (more specific `CaptureRenderedFrame`, `MouseDown`, `MouseMove` and `MouseUp`, found before Avalonia's own) make sure a frame has rendered first, because a slow Windows runner can lag. The user tests visuals on Windows and reports back with screenshots.

## Alpha performance work (1 November 2026)

The morph uses `AnimationFramePump`: at most one native frame request is outstanding across watchdog fallback, cancellation and restart. The watchdog advances without requesting an additional frame. Hiding or closing cancels the transition; completion runs once. The visual easing and durations remain unchanged.

Dashboard refresh requests made while hidden or animating are combined. The current workspace stays attached until the next reveal or expansion completion; tracking, saves and model notifications continue. Settings and Notes retain their existing caching rules. This does not suspend independent subscriptions inside child controls.

`PerformanceTimings` gathers bounded aggregate timings without requesting frames: startup data/model, window construction, warm-up Show, capture, transition preparation, first callback, callback gaps, total animation, save and workspace rebuild. Settings → Development → Copy report includes these with the frame monitor off. Callback times include watchdog steps and are not GPU presentation measurements. First-callback time starts when animation scheduling starts; preparation is measured separately. Startup timings are retained for the process lifetime.

Before judging smoothness, compare cold startup and repeated expand/collapse with the continuous frame monitor off, then use it separately for detailed frame observations. Check Timeline, Week and Notes; hide during a transition; quickly reopen; check Windows reduced motion and multiple display scales. Automated tests verify scheduler and refresh behaviour, not perceived smoothness on the user's GPU. Capture caching and deferred construction remain follow-up decisions based on these timings.

### Alpha performance follow-up (1 November 2026)

`TimerModel.CheckpointChange` distinguishes the routine 15-second save/refresh from edits. Dashboard refresh retains an idle timeline and any historical timeline/week on this notification. A running block on today's timeline and current-week totals still refresh at the original cadence; edits, navigation, theme, formatting, notes and selection continue to invalidate normally. `PresentationChange` covers pill-only settings, tooltip/developer settings and alert dismissal/snoozing without replacing the workspace. The totals bar retains its grid and segment controls, updating column widths and refreshing the stripe brush when its theme colour changes.

`TransitionTimings` adds the last 20 individual transitions to Copy report, with completion/cancellation, native callback count, watchdog count, native callback gaps and saves/rebuilds/preparation recorded during the transition. Native callbacks are not GPU presentation measurements. First callback is measured from animation scheduling; transition total starts before preparation and includes a deferred refresh at completion. The original aggregate animation total remains available for comparison with build 602. No continuous frame requests or disk logging are added.

This is conservative workspace reuse, not a full incremental renderer: active block geometry and current-week totals still rebuild periodically. Compare cold startup and repeated transitions with the continuous frame monitor off before deciding whether a snapshot transition or larger view refactor is justified.

An earlier week still refreshes if the running account has a monthly/quarterly budget whose period includes today; historical weekly totals alone are not sufficient to decide it is static.

### Snapshot transition comparison (Alpha)

Development settings offer Use dashboard snapshot / Use live dashboard. The default is live and the choice lasts only for the process. Try ten expand/collapse cycles per mode, with continuous frame timing off and the other diagnostic switches unchanged. Copy report after each set; each recent transition labels its mode. Snapshot mode captures the dashboard body at the current display scale once per transition, fades it with the existing easing and clip, and restores the live body on completion/cancellation. Setup/dialog overlays use live rendering. Capture failures fall back to live, labelled in the report. Captures are disposed when the transition ends.

The report includes dashboard capture cost, worst synchronous animation-update time, layout events, and a 20 ms Normal-priority dispatcher probe's worst lateness. The probe runs only during animated transitions in both modes and requests no frames. Lateness indicates delayed servicing of that priority, not necessarily application CPU work; OS scheduling and timer delivery also affect it. Native callbacks still do not measure GPU presentation. Compare total preparation cost as well as smoothness before choosing an implementation.

### Snapshot fidelity correction

The user's recording showed a zoomed/displaced snapshot followed by a snap back to the live layout. Dashboard snapshots now rasterize into a 96-DPI target with one explicit transform at the requested capture scale. They are drawn at fixed logical dimensions inside a Canvas layer, so bitmap intrinsic dimensions cannot change the dashboard's layout. Rendered-image regression coverage compares the live dashboard with its snapshot at 1, 1.25, 1.5 and 2 capture scales in Light and Dark. These are raster-scale comparisons; actual Windows per-monitor movement remains a native manual check.

Transition reports additionally time snapshot release, pill Show, dashboard Hide, pill activation and deferred refresh. This separates the collapse handoff from animation callbacks when investigating terminal UI stalls; no claim is made that those intermittent stalls have been resolved.

### First-frame transition alignment

Both pill and dashboard captures now use the explicit-scale 96-DPI raster path. The dashboard snapshot is initially transparent and explicitly measured/arranged at its logical body bounds before animation can expose it. Regression checks assert matching image bounds immediately (without settling) in both directions, and compare pill geometry at 1, 1.25, 1.5 and 2 capture scales. This addresses two code-level inconsistencies that could produce the remaining brief size flash; native confirmation is still needed before claiming the intermittent frame is eliminated.

### Startup preparation and default snapshot rendering

Snapshot rendering is now the normal animated transition; the temporary live/snapshot comparison setting is removed. Reduced motion, setup/dialog overlays and capture-failure fallback remain supported.

WindowCoordinator constructs the pill first. Passive state queries, taskbar updates, hide, reset, rescue and quit do not instantiate the dashboard. After the pill's first animation-frame callback, a Background-priority UI turn constructs the dashboard. Native off-screen warm-up remains a later turn (800 ms after construction), skipped if the dashboard has already been shown or the timer is hidden/closing. All view work stays on the UI thread. Explicit early requests use the same lazy instance synchronously before starting the morph. Fresh setup still opens the dashboard on first show. Bootstrap routing preserves Accounts, page, day and note requests arriving before dashboard construction.

Copy report now separates XAML/themes, storage open/load, model/demo construction, pill construction, dashboard construction, first dashboard request preparation and first request-to-callback. Launch-to-pill Opened/first callback and dashboard-prepared callback are measured from managed Program.Main entry, excluding OS/runtime work before Main; callbacks are not screen-presentation measurements. The older window construction figure now covers initial coordinator/pill wiring only. The work is moved off the initial pill path, not eliminated, and a click before warm-up can still pay preparation cost. Compare cold launch and immediate/delayed first dashboard opening before claiming a numerical speed-up.

### Hidden visual work and view lifetime

Notes cards/pages now ignore model-driven visual refreshes while an ancestor/window is hidden or the window is minimised. A ViewActivity observer refreshes them immediately on reveal/restore without clearing drafts or query state. Timeline clock and reminder-pin timers use the same attachment/visibility lifecycle: stop while inactive and catch up before restarting. The model tick, saving and reminder calculations remain active.

Dashboard refreshes are consolidated while minimised as well as hidden. Model/navigation subscriptions are removed on dashboard close. Coordinator front-check timers, screen subscriptions and cold-start request hooks stop on shutdown, and delayed rescue/warm-up work cannot revive it. FrameChip now resubscribes after reattachment. Native checks still pending are collected in docs/testing/performance-test-campaign.md for a later test session.

### Native reduced-motion cache

The initial reduced-motion read previously subtracted long.MinValue from TickCount64, overflowing and incorrectly accepting an uninitialised cache. The cache now checks its sentinel and monotonic time ordering first, and publishes the timestamp after the value is read. Native preference coverage compares unforced behaviour with Windows' client-area animation setting. Animation-specific visual tests explicitly choose enabled motion instead of inheriting CI desktop settings.

### Correctness guards before native feedback

AccountsDialog now pairs every kept source row with its edited AccountSettings before filtering rename pairs, so adding/reordering new rows cannot shift an existing account's identity. The regression exercises the dialog drag/save path and reloads persisted history, groups, notes and colours.

TickPaused suppresses routine visual Elapsed/checkpoint notifications only. Tick counters, date following, recurring-note generation, retry cadence and 15-second saves continue. Alert identity/light changes and checkpoint failures still notify; recovery clears the error through Save's existing notification. Diagnostic text describes this behaviour. The static diagnostic-pause test collection is isolated from other collections, and checks persisted ledger/running snapshots and failure/retry rather than relying on an unchanged snapshot save.

FrameMonitor callbacks carry a session id. A callback left pending by Stop cannot record or enqueue another frame after a restart or a switch to another window. Injected frame delivery makes both cases deterministic in regression tests. The campaign outline includes account identity and diagnostic-control checks.
